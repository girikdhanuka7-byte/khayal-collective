About

Why this exists

Many students carry stress about academics, friendships, identity, family pressure, and adjusting to school life. They may not always need formal counseling first, but they do need a safe first step.

Our mission is simple: make support easier to ask for, make listening easier to practice, and teach students how to disagree without harming each other.

How it works

Four steps from request to follow-up

01

Request support

Share what kind of help you need, how you want to be contacted, and whether you want to stay anonymous at first.

02

Get matched

A trained peer mentor is selected based on your topic, availability, and comfort preferences.

03

Talk

You meet in a supervised, boundaried support space. Mentors listen, ask good questions, and help you plan the next step.

04

Follow up

If needed, the team checks in again or helps connect you with a teacher, counselor, or trusted adult.

Debate Lab

Debate is taught as a wellbeing skill.

Students practice how to name a feeling, frame a claim, listen fairly, and disagree without attacking.

Practice motion

Schools should teach emotional literacy as seriously as exam skills.

Claim

Emotional literacy improves learning because students can manage stress before it becomes shutdown or conflict.

Evidence

Use examples from classroom experience, attendance patterns, peer support, or wellbeing surveys.

Respectful challenge

Ask: how can schools protect academic time while still teaching these skills?

Peer mentors

Who supports students?

Trained student volunteers

Mentors learn active listening, confidentiality rules, basic referral steps, and debate facilitation.

Clear role

They help students feel heard, organize thoughts, practice difficult conversations, and identify next steps.

Not therapists

Peer mentors are not professional therapists, counselors, or medical providers. Serious concerns are referred to trained adults.

Counselor support

Connect with a counselor when you need adult support.

Choose this option for heavier stress, safety concerns, ongoing emotional difficulty, or anything you do not want to handle through peer support.

When to choose counselor support

Use this path if you feel unsafe, overwhelmed, stuck for a long time, or need help from a trained adult. Peer mentors can listen, but counselors can provide formal school support.

Review safety boundaries

Safety and boundaries

Trust needs clear rules.

Confidentiality

Peer supporters keep conversations private within the program team, except when safety rules require help from a trusted adult.

What peers can do

Listen, help you sort thoughts, practice speaking up, share school resources, and support a next-step plan.

What peers cannot do

Diagnose, provide therapy, keep dangerous secrets, solve every problem, or replace counselors and guardians.

When we escalate

If there is risk of harm, abuse, serious crisis, or urgent safety concern, the team contacts a counselor, teacher, guardian, or emergency support according to school policy.
